A SMALL INTRODUCTION

The internet has enough dashboards.
Let’s make choosing fun.

Whimfate is an independent little project for the small crossroads of everyday life: the coin toss, the team split, the “what should we eat?” summit that could have been an emoji poll.

What are we doing here?

We are building a pocket-sized collection of browser-first tools for everyday randomness. The plan starts with six familiar things: splitting teams, a shared daily prompt, coin flips and dice, two-option dilemmas, random numbers, and a decision wheel.

They're deliberately small. Open the page, do the thing, get the result, get on with your day. The visual experience can have a little theatre; the actual task should not require a theatre degree.

Why the orb?

Because a plain grey rectangle labelled “Random Utility 04” does not exactly make the heart sing. The visual world can be a little theatrical; the tools themselves should be quick, clear, and easy to share. The orb is our mascot for uncertainty, not a prediction engine.

Browser-first, not account-first

The project is being built around lightweight experiences that work directly in a modern browser. Simple tasks should not need a login. Where practical, tools should avoid sending user-entered data anywhere that isn't necessary for their operation. The final behavior of each module, hosting, analytics, and any advertising will be documented honestly as the site grows.

What we are not

Whimfate is not a source of prophecy, a substitute for judgment, or a certified lottery system. Randomness can break a tie between two perfectly good snacks. It cannot make an unsafe or unsuitable option become a good one just because it landed on the wheel.

Still in the making

This site is the beginning, not a polished universe pretending to be finished. The first tool modules are being built one at a time. Until they arrive, the workbench cards are honest prototypes of what's planned — not buttons that lead to an empty room.

Got a tiny tool idea, a delightful use for randomness, or a case study in a group chat's inability to choose lunch? The maybe pile is a good place to start exploring.

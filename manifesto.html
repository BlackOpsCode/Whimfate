OUR HIGHLY UNOFFICIAL RULES

Less admin.
More accidental lore.

We believe small tools can be useful without feeling like tax software, and random outcomes can be fun without pretending to be messages from the universe.

01 — Solve the tiny problem. Quickly.

If someone opens a coin flipper, they probably want a coin flipped. They did not arrive hoping to configure 14 preferences, create an account, verify an email, and join a community called Heads Nation. Let the coin do its little job.

02 — Be useful, then be a little strange.

Good design does not have to wear a navy blazer. We like an orb with unnecessary cosmic gravitas. We like a result that looks good in a screenshot. We like a little glow. We also like buttons that say what they do and text you can read without squinting at the moon.

03 — If it is shareable, let it travel.

A team list should be easy to copy. A daily prompt should survive a trip into a group chat. A ridiculous result should be ready for the group chat before someone says “wait, screenshot that.” The fun should not be trapped in the browser like a tiny wizard in a jar.

04 — The orb is not a licensed professional.

Randomness is good for low-stakes choices: which game, which team, which snack, which of two perfectly acceptable plans. It is not how you choose a medical treatment, make a major financial decision, or determine whether your ex's vague message “means something.” Please keep your judgment switched on.

05 — Never confuse friction with depth.

No account wall for a simple task. No giant tutorial for a small button. No artificial urgency. No fake “AI-powered quantum destiny” unless the feature genuinely earns every one of those words. The goal is to help, not to make a coin flip sound like a graduate thesis.

06 — Give people a reason to come back.

Not with guilt, streaks that scold you, or a notification that says “the universe misses you.” Give people something new, useful, surprising, or amusing. A daily prompt should be worth a glance. A tool should save time. A story should earn the scroll.

07 — Make the result the star.

Give it a clear shape, a little personality, and a watermark so it can find its way home. Let the interface fade into the background once the fun part happens. If the best thing about a randomizer is the settings panel, we have accidentally built an admin tool.

Whimfate is not here to run your life. It's here for the small moments when nobody wants to pick the restaurant, the teams, or the first song.

That is the whole grand theory: less overthinking, more room for maybe. The orb may look important. It is still a sphere on the internet.
